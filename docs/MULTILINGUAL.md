# Multilingual Support — Strategic Value for OpenGrants

## Does multilingual add appeal & substance?

**Yes — significantly.**

Public-goods and grant funding is global. Major non-English ecosystems include:

- **Europe**: Horizon Europe, NLnet, Prototype Fund, national agencies (Germany, France, Netherlands, Nordic countries)
- **Latin America**: National science & technology funds, open-science initiatives
- **Asia**: Japan, South Korea, Singapore, India public research and open-source programs
- **Africa & Global South**: Growing public-interest technology and digital public goods funding

### Why it matters for OpenGrants specifically

1. **Discovery surface** — Many high-value opportunities never appear in English-only searches.
2. **Equity** — Open-source AI maintainers outside the Anglosphere face an even steeper discovery tax.
3. **Differentiation** — Almost no current grant/MCP tooling seriously supports multilingual discovery + drafting.
4. **Substance** — Real public-goods infrastructure must serve the global commons, not only English-speaking regions.

### Recommended phased approach

**Phase 1 (near-term)**  
- Accept and store non-English titles/descriptions without breaking ranking  
- Add language metadata on opportunities  
- Simple keyword expansion (e.g. “código abierto”, “open source”, “logiciel libre”)

**Phase 2**  
- Multilingual embedding / ranking  
- Drafting agent that can produce outlines in the user’s preferred language  
- Source adapters for major non-English portals

**Phase 3**  
- Full localization of the MCP tool descriptions and status resources  
- Community-maintained translation of the public-goods catalog

Adding multilingual capability moves OpenGrants from “excellent English-language tool” to “global public-goods funding infrastructure” — exactly the positioning needed to reach top 0.01% status.
